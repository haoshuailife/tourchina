Beijing is the political and cultural capital of China — 3,000 years of history, 21 million people, and gigabit internet in most apartments. For nomads, it offers the deepest cultural layer of any Chinese city: imperial palaces, surviving hutong alleyways, and a food tradition that goes far beyond Peking duck.

Here's how to actually live and work in Beijing as a foreign digital nomad.

## What makes Beijing different

Beijing is **not** the soft-landing city — that's Shanghai. Beijing rewards you for showing up curious. The hutongs still exist. The old men still play chess in the courtyards. The taxi drivers still don't speak English. But you're also in China's tech and startup capital (Zhongguancun, "China's Silicon Valley"), with some of the fastest residential internet in the country.

Come to Beijing if you want **depth**: language, history, and food. Go to Shanghai if you just want convenience.

## Top attractions you'll actually visit

### The Forbidden City (故宫)
![The Forbidden City viewed from Coal Hill in Beijing](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/The_Forbidden_City_-_View_from_Coal_Hill.jpg?width=1280 "The Forbidden City — Beijing's imperial palace, viewed from Jingshan Park")

The 9,999-room imperial palace at the heart of Beijing. Plan half a day minimum. **Book tickets online 7+ days in advance** — they sell out daily and the on-site booth doesn't sell to walk-ins anymore. Closed Mondays.

### The Great Wall — Mutianyu section
1.5 hours from the city, less crowded than Badaling and fully restored. Take the cable car up, toboggan down. Avoid weekends and Chinese public holidays. A private driver costs ~$60 round trip; public bus options exist but eat half a day in transit.

### Temple of Heaven (天坛)
![The Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests at the Temple of Heaven, Beijing](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Temple_of_Heaven_20160323_01.jpg?width=1280 "Temple of Heaven — where Ming and Qing emperors prayed for good harvests")

The Ming-dynasty circular altar where emperors prayed for good harvests. The surrounding park is where Beijingers do tai chi at 6am — go early to see the real morning culture.

### Summer Palace (颐和园)
Imperial summer escape with a vast lake, marble boat, and painted corridors. Rent a paddleboat in summer; ice-skate on the lake in January.

### The hutongs of Gulou and Nanluoguxiang
Narrow alleyways lined with courtyard homes (siheyuan), now mixed with cafés, bars, and boutiques. Best explored by bicycle on a weekday morning.

## Local customs to know

### Tea and face
The Beijing concept of "face" (mianzi) shapes everything. Don't publicly correct a Chinese host. Accept tea when offered. When paying, you may see two people literally wrestling for the bill — let them, or join in good-naturedly. Never split the bill silently; that's cold.

### The subway queue
Beijing's subway is punctual, cheap (~$0.50), and crowded. Stand right on escalators (the standing side is left in China — wait, it's actually right). Let passengers off before boarding. Security checkpoints at every station scan all bags.

### The "no tipping" rule
Tipping is not expected anywhere — not in restaurants, not in taxis, not in hotels. Adding a tip can confuse staff. Service charge is sometimes included at high-end hotels.

### Talking about politics
Beijingers are politically aware but cautious with foreigners. Don't initiate political topics. If a local raises one, listen politely. Avoid Tibet, Taiwan, Xinjiang, and 1989 unless a trusted local explicitly wants to talk.

## The food: what to actually eat

### Peking duck (北京烤鸭)
The signature dish. The whole duck is roasted over fruit-wood fire, carved at the table, and eaten in thin pancakes with scallion, cucumber, and sweet bean sauce.

**Where**:
- **Quanjude (全聚德)** — the original 1864 chain, tourist-heavy but reliable. ~$40/person.
- **Da Dong (大董)** — upscale modern interpretation. ~$60/person.
- **Siji Minfu (四季民福)** — local favorite, half the price of Quanjude. ~$25/person.

### Zhajiangmian (炸酱面)
Beijing's everyday noodle bowl — fresh wheat noodles topped with dark soybean paste, cucumber, and bean sprouts. A bowl costs $2-4 at any local noodle shop. The best ones are in the hutongs, not on Yelp.

### Lamb hotpot (涮羊肉)
Mongolian-style copper-pot hotpot with thin-sliced lamb, sesame paste dip, and a charcoal broth. Winter staple.

**Where**: **Donglaishun (东来顺)**, the century-old halal restaurant. ~$25/person.

### Jianbing (煎饼)
The Beijing breakfast crepe — egg, crispy cracker, scallion, and bean paste folded into a thin pancake. $1.50 from any street cart. Eat before 10am; carts vanish by lunch.

### Beijing-style dumplings (饺子)
Pork-and-chive or pork-and-cabbage. A plate of 15 costs $4-6. **Baoyuan Dumpling Shop** in Dongzhimen has 80+ fillings.

## Where to work: cafés and coworking

Beijing has a strong coworking scene, but café Wi-Fi quality varies. These have stable connections and outlets:

### Coworking spaces
| Space | Neighborhood | Day pass | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| WeWork Sanlitun | Sanlitun | ~$30/day | Largest, most foreigner-friendly |
| WeWork Guomao | CBD | ~$30/day | Finance crowd, glass towers |
| Naked Hub Gulou | Gulou | ~$25/day | Indie vibe, smaller |
| Impact Hub Beijing | various | ~$20/day | Community-focused, events |

### Cafés with Wi-Fi and outlets
- **Soloist Coffee** (multiple locations) — minimalist, fast Wi-Fi, foreigner-favorite
- **Seesaw Coffee** (Sanlitun, Guomao) — specialty roaster, reliable for video calls
- **% Arabica** (Sanlitun) — photo-friendly, but limited outlets — bring a power bank
- **Café Flatwhite** (Jing'an area) — Antipodean-style, outlets at most seats
- **Starbucks Reserve** (Sanlitun Taikoo Li) — large, quiet upstairs, free Wi-Fi

**Tip**: power outlets are not universal in Chinese cafés. Carry a 20,000 mAh power bank and assume you won't find an outlet.

## Best time to visit

| Season | Weather | Crowd | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spring (Apr–May) | Mild, dusty | Moderate | Best overall |
| Summer (Jun–Aug) | Hot, humid, rainy | High | Avoid |
| Autumn (Sep–Oct) | Cool, clear | Peak in Oct | Best, but book early |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | Cold, smog spikes | Low | Cheap, but wear a mask on bad AQI days |

## Weekend escapes

- **Great Wall at Mutianyu** — 1.5 hrs, cable car + toboggan
- **Chengde** — 3 hrs by train, imperial mountain summer palace
- **Tianjin** — 30 min bullet train, foodie day trip
- **Datong** — 3 hrs, Yungang Grottoes (1,500-year-old Buddhist caves)

## Bottom line

Beijing is for nomads who want **China, not a sanitized version of it**. The culture is deep, the food is world-class, and the internet is fast. Bring a VPN, a power bank, and an open mind — the city repays curiosity more than any other in China.
