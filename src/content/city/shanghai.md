Shanghai is the default entry city for foreign digital nomads in China. It's the most international, the most walkable, and the city where you can do everything in English if you must. The trade-off: it's the most expensive.

Here's how to actually live, work, and eat in Shanghai.

## Why Shanghai is the soft landing

Shanghai was a treaty port from 1842 to 1943, and that hybrid identity never left. Plane-tree-lined streets, Art Deco architecture, French place names, and a foreigner-friendly service layer that the rest of mainland China doesn't have. If you've never lived in China, Shanghai is where the friction is lowest.

## Top attractions

### The Bund (外滩)
![The Bund riverfront in Shanghai, with the Pudong skyline across the Huangpu River](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/The_Bund_2.jpg?width=1280 "The Bund — Shanghai's colonial-era riverfront at dusk")

Shanghai's riverfront promenade — colonial-era buildings on the west side, the futuristic Pudong skyline across the river. Best at night, when both banks light up. Walk south to north, take the Bund Sightseeing Tunnel (gimmicky but fun) under the river to Pudong.

### Yu Garden and City God Temple (豫园)
A classical Chinese garden in the Old City, surrounded by teahouses and tourist shops. Go early morning (opens 8:45am) to avoid crowds. The surrounding bazaar is where you'll find xiaolongbao.

### French Concession (法租界)
Not a single site but a whole neighborhood — the area the French administered from 1849 to 1943. Plane trees, lane houses, indie boutiques, and the densest café scene in Asia. **Walk Fuxing Road, Wukang Road, and Anfu Road** — that's the nomad afternoon.

### Shanghai Tower (上海中心大厦)
![Pudong skyline seen from across the Huangpu River on the Bund](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Shanghai_skyline_from_the_bund.jpg?width=1280 "Pudong skyline — home to the Shanghai Tower, China's tallest building")

China's tallest building (632m). The observation deck on the 118th floor costs ~$30. Go on a clear day; on smoggy days you'll see only cloud.

### Zhujiajiao Water Town (朱家角)
A 1,700-year-old canal town on the edge of Shanghai — 1 hour by subway. Stone bridges, Ming-dynasty houses, street food. A perfect half-day escape.

## Local customs to know

### The "queue" is optional
Shanghai is more orderly than Beijing but less than Tokyo. Subway boarding is roughly a queue. Escalators: stand right, walk left. Don't be shocked if an older local cuts — patience wins.

### Gift-giving at meetings
If you meet a Chinese business contact, a small gift from your home country (chocolates, a craft item) earns points. Two hands when giving and receiving. Don't give clocks (the word sounds like "attending a funeral") or anything in sets of four.

### Drinking culture
Shanghai business drinking is **baijiu** (clear spirit, 40-60% ABV) at banquets. Pace yourself. It's acceptable to cite health reasons to skip. Beer is fine. Never pour your own drink first — pour for others, they'll pour for you.

### The Shanghai dialect
Shanghainese (上海话) is mutually unintelligible with Mandarin. Most people under 50 speak Mandarin; older locals may prefer Shanghainese. Don't try to learn Shanghainese — Mandarin is the only useful language.

## The food: what to actually eat

### Xiaolongbao (小笼包)
Shanghai's signature — soup-filled pork dumplings, steamed in bamboo baskets. Bite the top, sip the broth, dip in black vinegar with ginger.

**Where**:
- **Jia Jia Tang Bao (佳家汤包)** — local chain, $5 for a basket, the people's choice
- **Din Tai Fung** — Taiwanese upscale, ~$15 for the basket, consistent quality
- **Fu Chun (富春)** — 70-year-old Hangzhou-style, lighter broth

### Shengjianbao (生煎包)
Pan-fried soup buns — crispy bottom, fluffy top, hot soup inside. The breakfast of Shanghai.

**Where**: **Da Hu Chun (大壶春)** — the original since 1932, ~$3 for a plate.

### Hongshao rou (红烧肉)
Red-braised pork belly — dark, sweet, soy-lacquer glaze. The signature home-style dish.

**Where**: **Old Shanghai Restaurant (老饭店)** on Fuyou Road.

### Hairy crab (大闸蟹)
October–December specialty. Eat with ginger, black vinegar, and warm huangjiu (yellow wine). Getting the meat out is a 30-minute art. **Avoid the gills.**

### Congyoubing (葱油饼)
Scallion oil flatbread — flaky, crispy, $1 from street carts in the morning.

## Where to work: cafés and coworking

Shanghai has the best café scene in mainland China. Wi-Fi is generally stable.

### Coworking spaces
| Space | Neighborhood | Day pass | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| WeWork Shanghai | Jing'an, Xintiandi | ~$35/day | Multiple floors, strong expat network |
| Naked Hub | Xintiandi, People's Square | ~$28/day | Design-forward, smaller rooms |
| Distrii | Lujiazui, Xintiandi | ~$25/day | Chinese tech crowd |
| People2 | Jing'an | ~$22/day | Boutique, events-heavy |

### Cafés with Wi-Fi and outlets
- **Manner Coffee** (60+ locations) — Shanghai-grown, $3 flat whites, fast Wi-Fi, often standing-only — go early
- **Seesaw Coffee** (multiple) — specialty roaster, café-style seating
- **Mello Coffee** (French Concession) — quiet, designed for working, outlets at most seats
- **% Arabica** (Wukang Road) — beautiful, photo-famous, limited seating
- **Starbucks Reserve Roastery** (Nanjing Road) — enormous, touristy but reliable for an afternoon

## Getting connected

VPN setup is the same nationwide — see [VPN in China](/guides/vpn-china).

Local SIM cards are easiest to buy in Shanghai — China Mobile stores are everywhere, English service at major branches. Or skip the line with [eSIM for China](/guides/esim-china).

Set up [WeChat Pay](/guides/wechat-pay-foreigners) within your first day. Shanghai is cashless — even street carts take it.

## Best time to visit

| Season | Weather | Crowd | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spring (Mar–May) | Mild, rainy | Moderate | Good |
| Plum rain (Jun–mid-Jul) | Wet, humid | Moderate | Tough |
| Late summer (Aug) | Hot, typhoons | High | Avoid |
| Autumn (Sep–Nov) | Cool, dry | Peak | Best |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | Cold, no central heat | Low | Cheap, indoor cold |

## Weekend escapes

- **Hangzhou** — 45 min by bullet train, West Lake, tea villages
- **Suzhou** — 25 min, classical gardens, canals
- **Water towns** (Zhujiajiao, Xitang, Tongli) — 1 hr, day trips
- **Moganshan** — 2.5 hrs, bamboo forests, expat boutique hotels

## Bottom line

Shanghai is the city to start in if you've never nomaded in China. Get your VPN, eSIM, and WeChat Pay wired up in your first 72 hours. Once those three are working, the city becomes one of the easiest places in Asia to live and work.
