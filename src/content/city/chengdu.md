Chengdu is the nomad secret of southwest China. It's relaxed, affordable, food-obsessed, and the gateway to Sichuan, Tibet, and Yunnan. Most nomads who try Chengdu end up staying longer than planned.

## What makes Chengdu different

Chengdu has **no rush**. Locals call it "the city where you come to play" (耍都). Teahouses line the parks. People linger over lunch. The pace is half of Shanghai's. Yet underneath the slow surface, Chengdu is one of China's biggest gaming-industry hubs (Tencent's largest office is here), with fast internet and a booming tech scene.

Cost is the killer feature: roughly **40% cheaper than Shanghai** for the same nomad lifestyle.

## Top attractions

### Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding (成都大熊猫繁育研究基地)
![A giant panda resting at the Chengdu Research Base](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Grosser_Panda.JPG?width=1280 "Giant panda at the Chengdu Research Base — go early to catch them active")

The world's largest panda research facility. 80+ pandas. Go at 8am — feeding time, pandas active. By afternoon they're sleeping. ~$15 entry. 30 min from city center by metro + shuttle.

### Jinli Ancient Street (锦里) and Wuhou Shrine (武侯祠)
Reconstructed ancient market street next to the Three Kingdoms-era shrine. Tourist-heavy but atmospheric — try the street food, see the lanterns at night.

### Wide and Narrow Alleys (宽窄巷子)
Qing-era alleyways restored into a tourist zone of teahouses, craft shops, and bars. More polished than Beijing's hutongs, more accessible to foreigners.

### Wenshu Monastery (文殊院)
A working Buddhist temple from the Tang dynasty. Free entry. Tea in the courtyard is the most peaceful hour you'll have in Chengdu.

### People's Park (人民公园)
The real Chengdu — locals playing mahjong, drinking tea, matchmakers with marriage proposals on umbrellas. The Heming Teahouse here has been operating for 100+ years.

## Local customs to know

### Tea house culture
Teahouses are Chengdu's living room. You pay ~$2 for a cup that gets refilled all afternoon. Mahjong is everywhere — learn the clatter, don't try to play unless invited. Sitting and doing nothing for 3 hours is normal here.

### The Sichuan spice contract
Sichuan food is numbing-spicy (málà, 麻辣). The Sichuan peppercorn doesn't burn — it numbs your lips and enhances other flavors. **When a local says "this is mild," they mean local mild.** Ask for "wei la" (微辣) at restaurants and you'll still sweat.

### Slow time
Chengdu time is different. A 2pm lunch meeting might mean 3pm. A "quick tea" can run 4 hours. Don't fight it. Bring a book and your laptop. The slowness is the point.

### Bargaining at markets
Jinli and tourist zones have inflated prices. Bargain down 50-70%. Smile, walk away, the price drops. At fixed-price stores (supermarkets, chains, malls) prices are firm.

## The food: what to actually eat

### Sichuan hotpot (四川火锅)
The thing to do in Chengdu. A vat of bubbling red oil with Sichuan peppercorns floating on top. You cook raw ingredients in it.

**Where**:
- **Shu Jiu Xiang (蜀九香)** — local mid-range, ~$20/person
- **Hai Di Lao (海底捞)** — famous chain, theatrical service, ~$25/person
- **Long Niu (龙吴火锅)** — hole-in-the-wall, $12/person, the real experience

**Order**: beef slices, lotus root, enoki mushrooms, tripe, fish balls, and **a sesame oil dip bowl** (the standard). Ask for "yuanyang" pot (鸳鸯, half-spicy, half-mild broth) if you need a break.

### Mapo tofu (麻婆豆腐)
The Sichuan classic — silken tofu in a sauce of chili, fermented bean paste, and Sichuan peppercorn. **Chen Mapo Tofu (陈麻婆豆腐)** is the original since 1862.

### Kung Pao chicken (宫保鸡丁)
Yes, the dish you know — but here it's actually good. Diced chicken, peanuts, dried chilies, slightly sweet-sour. Found at any Sichuan restaurant.

### Dan dan noodles (担担面)
Wheat noodles in a spicy sesame-chili sauce with minced pork. The street version costs $1.50.

### Chuan chuan (串串)
Skewers of meat and veg cooked in boiling broth — like hotpot but on sticks. Cheap, social, perfect with beer. ~$8 for a big feed.

### Rabbit head (兔头)
The local delicacy. Yes, a rabbit's head, braised in spices. Look intimidating; tastes like spicy chicken wing. ~$3 each. Try once.

## Where to work: cafés and coworking

Chengdu's coworking scene is smaller than Shanghai's but adequate. Café culture is growing fast.

### Coworking spaces
| Space | Neighborhood | Day pass | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| WeWork | various | ~$25/day | Multiple locations |
| Distrii | Tianfu Square area | ~$20/day | Chinese tech crowd |
| Local independents near Software Park | South Chengdu | $10-15/day | Gaming industry density |

### Cafés with Wi-Fi and outlets
- **Manner Coffee** (multiple in Chunxi Road, Tianfu Square) — Shanghai-grown, fast Wi-Fi
- **Seesaw Coffee** (IFS mall) — specialty, reliable for calls
- **% Arabica** (Sino-Ocean Taikoo Li) — photo-famous, limited seating
- **The Bookworm** (Tianfu Square) — bookshop café, expat hangout, slower Wi-Fi but quiet
- **Starbucks Reserve** (IFS mall) — large, quiet upstairs

**Tip**: teahouses have Wi-Fi but rarely outlets — best for reading and slow work, not all-day laptop sessions.

## Weekend escapes

- **Panda Base** — 30 min, go at 8am
- **Leshan Giant Buddha** — 1.5 hrs, the world's largest stone Buddha (71m)
- **Mount Emei (峨眉山)** — 1.5 hrs, sacred Buddhist peak
- **Jiuzhaigou** — alpine lakes, 8 hrs by car or short flight
- **Lhasa (Tibet)** — train boards here; arrange permits with a tour operator

## Bottom line

Chengdu is for nomads who want **China without the coastal price tag and hustle**. Come for a week, leave in three months. Bring a VPN, bring antacids for the spice, and a flexible return ticket.
