An eSIM is the fastest way to get online after landing in China. No SIM card shop queue, no passport paperwork — scan a QR code before you fly, tap "Add eSIM" on landing, and you're online in 30 seconds.

Here's the exact process that works in 2026.

## Step 1: Check phone compatibility

Not all phones support eSIM. Confirmed working in China:

| Phone | eSIM support? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| iPhone XS, XR, XS Max and newer | ✅ | **Except iPhones sold in mainland China** — those have eSIM disabled |
| iPhone SE (2020+) and 14/15/16 (non-China) | ✅ | |
| Google Pixel 3 and newer | ✅ | All markets |
| Samsung Galaxy S20+ and newer flagships | ✅ | Region-dependent — verify on Samsung's site |
| Samsung Galaxy S21 FE, S22/S23/S24 (non-US) | ✅ | Some US carrier-locked versions don't |
| Motorola Razr, Edge+ | ✅ | |
| Most phones sold in mainland China | ❌ | eSIM disabled by Chinese regulation |

**Critical**: If you bought your phone inside mainland China, eSIM won't work for you. Plan on a physical SIM instead.

Check your phone: Settings → Cellular → Add Cellular Plan (iOS) or Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs → Add eSIM (Android). If the option exists, your phone supports eSIM.

## Step 2: Choose an international travel eSIM

Chinese carriers (China Mobile, Unicom, Telecom) don't sell eSIM plans to non-residents. Tourists must use international travel eSIMs that **roam** on Chinese networks.

## Step 3: Buy and install before your flight

1. Download the provider's app on your phone
2. Pick a plan (start with 5GB if it's your first week — you can top up)
3. Pay with credit card or Apple/Google Pay
4. The app shows a QR code — **screenshot it and save to photos**
5. On iPhone: Settings → Cellular → Add Cellular Plan → scan QR code
6. On Android: Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs → Add eSIM → scan QR code
7. **Name the plan** "China eSIM" so you can find it later
8. **Toggle it OFF** for now — don't activate until you land (most plans start the 30-day clock on first connection)

## Step 4: Configure data routing

By default, your phone may try to use your home SIM for data. You want the eSIM for data in China (it's cheaper than roaming your home SIM).

On iPhone:
- Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data → select **China eSIM**
- Settings → Cellular → Default Voice Line → keep your home SIM (for calls)

On Android:
- Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs → **Mobile data** → China eSIM

This routes all data through the eSIM and keeps calls/SMS on your home SIM.

## Step 5: Landing — activate in 30 seconds

1. After the seatbelt sign turns off, **toggle the eSIM on** in Settings
2. Wait 20-30 seconds for it to register on China Mobile/Unicom
3. Confirm data works by opening any website (note: Google/WhatsApp still blocked without a VPN — see Step 6)
4. Use Maps.me, Apple Maps, or Baidu Maps (Baidu works best in China) for navigation

You're now online — no airport SIM queue, no passport scan, no Chinese-language forms.

## Step 6: Handle the Great Firewall

The big surprise for most nomads: **the Great Firewall applies to your eSIM data too**, because the eSIM roams on a Chinese carrier network.

What this means:
- **WhatsApp, Google, Instagram, YouTube, OpenAI** — all still blocked
- Some international eSIMs route through Hong Kong/Singapore and **sometimes** let WhatsApp through inconsistently
- **For reliable access, run your VPN** (see our [VPN in China guide](/guides/vpn-china))

**Apps that work without a VPN in China**:
- Baidu Maps (Chinese only, but functional)
- DiDi (Chinese Uber)
- WeChat (after setup)
- Alipay / WeChat Pay (after setup)
- Apple Maps (in China, it uses AutoNavi data — works fine)
- Maps.me with offline maps

## Step 7: Plan the transition to a local SIM

An eSIM is a bridge, not a long-term data plan. After your first week:

1. **Visit a China Mobile or Unicom store** (China Telecom has less English support)
2. Bring your **passport** and a copy of your hotel registration
3. Ask for a tourist SIM ("旅游卡, lǚyóu kǎ")
4. Plans: ~$15-25/month for 10-20GB, much cheaper than eSIM roaming
5. The store will activate it on the spot — takes 20-30 minutes

Keep your eSIM as backup for trips to Hong Kong, Vietnam, and other countries.

## Common pitfalls

### "My phone supports eSIM, so it'll work in China"
Some carriers in some regions disable eSIM activation on locked phones. Unlock your phone with your home carrier before traveling.

### Buying the eSIM in the airport
Airport Wi-Fi is monitored and the QR code requires internet to install. **Buy and install at home before flying.**

### Expecting the eSIM to bypass the Great Firewall
Some marketing implies this — it's mostly false. International eSIMs may let WhatsApp through, but Google/YouTube/OpenAI almost always need a VPN.

### Forgetting to switch data routing
If you keep your home SIM as the data line, you'll pay $10/MB in roaming fees. **Always set the eSIM as the data line** (Step 4).

## Before you fly — final checklist

- [ ] Phone supports eSIM (verified in Settings)
- [ ] Phone unlocked by home carrier
- [ ] International eSIM purchased and installed (QR code scanned)
- [ ] eSIM toggled OFF until landing (to not start the 30-day clock)
- [ ] Data routing pre-configured to eSIM
- [ ] VPN installed separately on the same phone (for Great Firewall)
- [ ] Offline maps downloaded (Apple Maps, Maps.me, or Baidu)

Done correctly, an eSIM gets you online before you've left the airport gate. Done wrong, you'll spend your first afternoon in a SIM card queue.
