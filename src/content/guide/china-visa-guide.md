China's visa rules loosened significantly in 2024-2026. Many nomads now enter **visa-free** for short trips, and longer stays are easier than they've been in a decade. This is the practical map for 2026.

## Step 1: Identify which entry option fits your trip

China offers four entry paths for nomads in 2026. Pick by passport and trip length.

### Option A: Visa-free entry (no application)

China now offers **30-day visa-free entry** to citizens of dozens of countries, including most of the EU, Malaysia, Australia, and others. The list expands regularly.

- **Best for**: scouting trips up to 30 days
- **Cost**: $0
- **Application**: none
- **Requirement**: passport valid 6+ months, onward ticket (sometimes asked)

### Option B: 240-hour transit visa exemption

If your country isn't on the visa-free list, you may qualify for **240-hour (10-day) transit visa exemption**:

- **Requirement**: onward ticket to a **third country** (not your origin country)
- **Entry/exit**: through approved ports (major airports, Shenzhen-HK border)
- **Region limit**: you cannot leave the registered transit region (e.g., Beijing-only transit limits you to Beijing area)
- **Best for**: a 7-10 day scouting trip

### Option C: L Visa (standard tourist visa)

The classic tourist visa, valid 30/60/90 days per entry. Apply at a Chinese embassy or visa center.

- **Single entry**: 30-90 days
- **Double entry**: 2 entries within 6 months
- **Multiple entry**: valid 6 months to 10 years depending on passport
- **Best for**: 1-3 month stays, multi-entry flexibility for visa runs

### Option D: M Visa (business) or F Visa (exchange)

Less common for nomads, but if you're setting up a Chinese company or have a Chinese business partner, the M visa allows business activity and longer stays. **Not recommended** for casual remote work — technically borderline.

## Step 2: Check your passport's eligibility

Before booking flights, check whether your passport qualifies for the easiest options:

1. Visit your nearest **Chinese embassy's website** (find yours at `visaforchina.org`)
2. Search "visa-free entry list 2026" or "transit visa exemption"
3. The list of qualifying countries changes — verify current status, don't rely on 2024 articles
4. If your passport qualifies for visa-free entry: skip to Step 5
5. If not: continue to Step 3

## Step 3: Apply for the L Visa (if needed)

If you need a tourist visa, the application process:

### Documents required

- [ ] Passport valid 6+ months beyond your stay, with 2+ blank pages
- [ ] Passport photo (48×33mm, white background, no glasses)
- [ ] Completed visa application form (online via `visaforchina.org`)
- [ ] Round-trip flight booking (or onward ticket)
- [ ] Hotel booking for first nights, or invitation letter from a host
- [ ] Proof of funds (sometimes asked — bank statement with $5,000+ equivalent)
- [ ] Previous China visas (if you've been before — improves approval)

### Where to apply

1. Find your nearest Chinese Visa Application Service Center (CVASC) at `visaforchina.org`
2. Book an appointment online
3. Submit documents in person (some countries now accept mail-in)
4. Pay the visa fee (~$80-160 USD depending on country and processing speed)
5. Wait 2-4 weeks (standard) or 2-4 days (express, +$30)

### Visa type and validity

Request what fits your travel pattern:

| Visa type | Validity | Entries | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-entry L | 3 months | 1 | One trip under 90 days |
| Double-entry L | 6 months | 2 | One visa-run trip |
| Multi-entry L (1 year) | 1 year | Multiple | Frequent traveler |
| Multi-entry L (10 year) | 10 years | Multiple | US/Canada/Australia passports |

Even short nomad stays benefit from multi-entry if you plan Hong Kong/Vietnam visa runs.

## Step 4: Book flights and accommodation

For L visa approval and border entry, you need:

- **Round-trip flight booking**: shows you intend to leave. Use a refundable booking if you don't know your return date.
- **Hotel booking for first 3+ nights**: any major booking site works. Cancel after visa approval if you want flexibility.
- **Printed copies** of both — border agents occasionally ask.

## Step 5: Prepare for border entry

At check-in for your flight to China, the airline may ask:
- Passport (with visa if applicable)
- Printed visa approval (if L visa)
- Onward flight ticket
- Hotel booking for first nights

At Chinese immigration:
- Have your passport and visa ready
- Have the address of your first hotel written down (English is fine)
- Have a return ticket printed
- Answer questions briefly and honestly
- **Don't** volunteer that you're working remotely — if asked, "tourism" is the safe answer

## Step 6: Extend or change visa status inside China (if needed)

If you want to stay longer than your visa allows:

1. Visit a local **Entry-Exit Administration Bureau** (出入境管理处)
2. Apply at least **7 days before** your current visa expires
3. Bring: passport, registration form from your local police station, proof of funds, explanation letter
4. Extension is usually granted 30 extra days
5. Cost: ~$30

Don't overstay. Fines start at ~$15/day, plus possible detention and a 1-5 year ban from re-entering China.

## Step 7: Plan visa runs (for long stays)

A common nomad pattern: a 90-day L visa, do a run to Hong Kong or Vietnam when it expires, re-enter for another 90 days.

### Visa run checklist

- [ ] Book a same-day or overnight trip to Hong Kong, Hanoi, or Bangkok
- [ ] Print your return ticket to China
- [ ] Have your visa (or visa-free eligibility) confirmed for re-entry
- [ ] Bring proof of accommodation in China for the next stay
- [ ] Have a clear story if the border agent asks ("tourism" or "visiting friends")

Border agents occasionally question repeat entries. Have a clear, honest story and onward tickets. Repeat-entry tolerance varies — some agents don't care, others get suspicious after 3 entries in 6 months.

## Quick reference: visa options by trip length

| Trip length | Recommended option |
|---|---|
| 1-7 days scouting | 240-hour transit visa exemption |
| 1-30 days | Visa-free entry (if eligible) |
| 1-3 months | L Visa (single entry) |
| 3-6 months + visa runs | L Visa (double or multi-entry) |
| 6+ months frequent travel | Multi-entry L Visa (1 or 10 year) |

## Common pitfalls

### "I'll sort the visa at the airport"
China does **not** offer true visa-on-arrival for tourists. The Shenzhen 5-day VOA is the only exception and is limited. Sort your visa before flying.

### Booking non-refundable flights before visa approval
If your visa is rejected, you lose the flight money. Use a refundable booking for visa application, switch to non-refundable after approval.

### Trusting 2023 visa-free lists
The list expands regularly. Some countries added in 2024-2025 may have specific conditions. Verify at the official source.

### "I'll work remotely, that's fine"
Strictly, it's a gray area. Don't volunteer this information to border agents or visa officers. "Tourism" is the safe answer. **Never** do client work for Chinese clients on a tourist visa — that's a clear violation.

## Before you fly — final checklist

- [ ] Passport valid 6+ months beyond stay, with 2+ blank pages
- [ ] Visa type selected based on trip length and passport
- [ ] L Visa applied for (if needed) and approved
- [ ] Round-trip or onward flight booked (refundable until visa approved)
- [ ] Hotel booking for first 3+ nights
- [ ] Printed copies of all documents
- [ ] Travel insurance (sometimes asked; always smart)

Don't overstay — fines are real and bans is 1-5 years. If you need more time, apply for an extension 7+ days before expiry.
